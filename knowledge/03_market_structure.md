# Market Structure & Price Action

> Synthesized from Dow Theory, Wyckoff Method, Smart Money Concepts (ICT), and Al Brooks price action.

This is how to *read* the chart. Market structure gives you context — it answers "where are we in the cycle?" before you even look at indicators.

---

## The Core Principle

> Price is the only truth. Indicators are derivatives of price, lagging and smoothed. Price action shows you what's happening *right now*.

Structure shows you the skeleton. Indicators should only confirm what structure already tells you.

---

## Dow Theory Basics — The Foundation

Markets trend in **higher highs + higher lows (uptrend)** or **lower highs + lower lows (downtrend)**. Anything else is a range.

### The Three Trends (timeframes)

1. **Primary (HTF)**: Weekly/Daily. The dominant direction. Trade with it.
2. **Secondary (MTF)**: 4H/1H. Pullbacks and counter-moves within the primary trend. Entries here.
3. **Minor (LTF)**: 15M/5M. Noise. Execution timing only.

**Rule**: entries should align higher timeframe trend and lower timeframe setup.

## Wyckoff Market Cycle — The Four Phases

Every market moves through the same four phases. Identifying which phase you're in determines strategy:

### Phase 1: Accumulation
- After a markdown. Price ranges sideways at lows.
- Smart money quietly accumulates from weak hands.
- Volume: declining overall, with spikes on dips (absorption).
- **Look for**: "Spring" — a false breakdown below support, followed by a strong reversal.

### Phase 2: Markup
- Price breaks out of accumulation and trends up.
- Volume expands on rallies, contracts on pullbacks.
- **Best phase to trade long.** Buy pullbacks, ride the trend.

### Phase 3: Distribution
- After a markup. Price ranges at highs.
- Smart money quietly distributes to retail buyers.
- Volume: high with price flat (effort without result).
- **Look for**: "Upthrust" — a false breakout above resistance, followed by breakdown.

### Phase 4: Markdown
- Price breaks down from distribution and trends down.
- Volume expands on selloffs, contracts on bounces.
- **Best phase to trade short.** Sell rallies, ride the downtrend.

### Why Wyckoff Works Especially Well in Crypto
- Whale-driven: a handful of large holders control most of the float.
- Leverage amplifies phases: springs and upthrusts are violent and obvious.
- Clean cycles: Bitcoin's 4-year halving cycle produces textbook Wyckoff patterns.

## Key Market Structure Concepts

### Break of Structure (BOS)
- Price breaks a recent significant **high in an uptrend** (or low in a downtrend).
- Confirms trend *continuation*.
- Bullish BOS: new higher high → uptrend intact. Look for pullback entries.

### Change of Character (ChoCH)
- First sign of trend reversal.
- In uptrend: price breaks the *most recent higher low* → trend may be shifting.
- In downtrend: price breaks the *most recent lower high* → trend may be shifting.
- **ChoCH alone is not an entry signal.** It's a warning to stop trading the old trend.

### Liquidity Sweep / Stop Hunt
- Price wicks past an obvious support/resistance level (e.g., previous swing low) then reverses.
- The wick's purpose: trigger stop losses resting below the level.
- Smart money uses these stops as liquidity to enter opposite positions.
- **Best reversal signal in crypto.** Wicks below obvious lows often mark bottoms.

### Order Blocks
- The last opposite-color candle before a strong move.
- Example: last bearish candle before a strong bullish move → that candle's range is a bullish order block.
- Price often returns to test this zone before continuing the move.
- Used as entry zone with stop on the far side.

### Fair Value Gap (FVG) / Imbalance
- Three-candle pattern where the middle candle's range doesn't overlap with the wicks of the neighboring candles.
- Represents an inefficiency — price moved so fast it left a gap.
- Price tends to return to fill these gaps.
- Useful as pullback target or reversal zone.

## Support & Resistance — The Basics

### Horizontal S/R
- Price levels where supply/demand previously shifted.
- Drawn from **swing highs/lows**, not random levels.
- Stronger when tested multiple times *and* aligns with round numbers or prior all-time highs/lows.

### Dynamic S/R (Moving Averages, Trendlines)
- 50 EMA and 200 EMA on daily: institutional reference levels.
- 20 EMA on 4H: short-term trend filter.
- Trendlines: draw from 2+ significant touches, extend forward.

### The Flip
- Broken support becomes resistance.
- Broken resistance becomes support.
- This "flip" is one of the highest-probability re-entry zones.

### Fibonacci Retracement Levels
- Measured from a significant swing low to swing high (uptrend) or swing high to swing low (downtrend).
- Key levels:
  - **0.382 (38.2%)**: shallow retracement — strong trend, buyers/sellers stepping in early.
  - **0.5 (50%)**: midpoint — not a true Fibonacci number but widely watched.
  - **0.618 (61.8%) — the "Golden Pocket"**: the most important retracement level. Institutional re-accumulation zone. Where the strongest pullback entries occur.
  - **0.786 (78.6%)**: deep retracement — last stand before full reversal. If this breaks, the move is likely fully retraced.
- **Golden pocket zone (0.618–0.65)**: the highest-probability reversal area. When price retraces to this zone after an impulsive move, expect a strong reaction. Use as:
  - Target for mean reversion / exhaustion setups (e.g., Setup 8 ATH Exhaustion T1)
  - Entry zone for trend continuation after a pullback
- **How to draw**: identify the swing low that initiated the move and the swing high where it ended. Apply Fibonacci from low to high (uptrend) or high to low (downtrend). The tool measures the percentage retracement between those points.
- **In crypto**: Fibonacci levels are self-fulfilling because so many participants watch them. The golden pocket is especially reliable on 4H and 1D timeframes.
- **Confluence**: a Fibonacci level that aligns with horizontal S/R, an EMA, or an order block is significantly stronger than a standalone Fibonacci level.

## Candlestick Patterns That Matter

Forget 60+ patterns. Focus on these:

### Reversal signals (at S/R levels only)
- **Hammer / Pin bar**: long wick rejecting a level, small body. Strong signal at support.
- **Shooting star / Inverted hammer**: opposite of above, at resistance.
- **Engulfing**: large candle whose body engulfs the previous candle's body. Especially strong with volume.

### Continuation signals
- **Three strong candles same direction**: momentum is intact.
- **Inside bar**: small candle inside the previous large candle — consolidation before continuation.

### Warning signs
- **Doji at an extreme**: indecision after a strong move. Often precedes reversal.
- **Long wick in direction of trend**: buyers/sellers losing control.

## Multi-Timeframe Analysis (MTFA)

**This is mandatory.** Never enter a trade based on only one timeframe.

### The Three-Timeframe Approach

1. **Higher TF (HTF)** — the "why":
   - Daily/Weekly.
   - Determines primary trend direction, major S/R levels.
   - Entries must align with HTF bias.

2. **Middle TF (MTF)** — the "where":
   - 4H/1H.
   - Identifies swing structure, order blocks, pullback zones.
   - Setup forms here.

3. **Lower TF (LTF)** — the "when":
   - 15M/5M.
   - Execution timing — wait for LTF confirmation (ChoCH, BOS, pin bar at MTF level).

### Rule: if MTF and HTF conflict → no trade.
If daily is down but 4H shows a bounce setup, the trade has a limited runway and poor R:R.

## Trend Identification Checklist

Ask these in order:

1. **What's the higher high / higher low structure on daily?** (Direction of primary trend)
2. **Where are we relative to major S/R?** (Zone of interest)
3. **Is volume confirming the trend?** (Rising volume with direction)
4. **Are moving averages aligned?** (Price above 50 & 200 EMA for uptrend)
5. **What does the 4H show?** (Pullback in trend, or range?)

If answers align → high-probability setup territory. If mixed → either skip or wait for clarity.

## Specific High-Probability Setup Structures

### Setup 1: Trend Pullback (Highest win rate)
- **Context**: clean HTF uptrend, price above 50 EMA.
- **Entry trigger**: pullback to 50 EMA or prior resistance-turned-support, with bullish LTF confirmation (ChoCH up or pin bar).
- **Stop**: below the swing low of the pullback.
- **Target**: previous swing high, or 1.5-2x the pullback depth projected.

### Setup 2: Range Breakout with Volume
- **Context**: clear horizontal range for 20+ candles, volume compressing.
- **Entry trigger**: close above range high on 2x+ average volume, or retest of broken resistance.
- **Stop**: below range midpoint.
- **Target**: range height projected above breakout.
- **Warning**: fake breakouts common. Prefer the retest entry over the initial breakout.

### Setup 3: Wyckoff Spring Reversal
- **Context**: clear accumulation range after a downtrend, 15-30+ candles at support.
- **Entry trigger**: false breakdown below range low, followed by strong reclaim of the range.
- **Stop**: below the spring low.
- **Target**: range high, then 1-2× range height above.
- **Highest R:R setup** — often 1:3 to 1:5.

### Setup 4: Liquidity Sweep Reversal
- **Context**: clear swing high/low that retail stops sit beyond.
- **Entry trigger**: wick beyond the level then LTF ChoCH back into range.
- **Stop**: just beyond the wick extreme.
- **Target**: opposite side of range, or prior structure.

## What NOT to Trade

- Choppy ranges with no clear direction (wait for resolution)
- First 15 minutes after major news (volatility, false moves)
- Directly into major S/R without confirmation
- "Looks like" a pattern but timeframes don't align
- Low-volume periods (typically weekend lows in crypto)
- After 3+ failed attempts at a level (market is showing weakness for both sides)

## How Claude Should Apply This

When analyzing technicals for the brief:

1. **State HTF trend first** (daily). All setups must be reconciled against this.
2. **Identify current Wyckoff phase** where applicable. This changes strategy:
   - Accumulation → look for springs
   - Markup → look for pullback entries
   - Distribution → look for upthrusts / exit longs
   - Markdown → look for bounce-short entries
3. **For every setup, provide**: entry zone (not single price), invalidation (stop loss), target(s), R:R.
4. **Prefer setups that align 2+ structural factors**: e.g., "pullback to 50 EMA + bullish order block + ChoCH up" beats "pullback to 50 EMA alone."
5. **Down-weight setups** where price is in the middle of a range with no clear context.
