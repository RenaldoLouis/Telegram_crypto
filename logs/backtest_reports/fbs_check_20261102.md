## Reading (post-hoc, written after the numbers — not part of the pre-registered verdict)

- **The verdict line is NOT AN EDGE by the letter of the rule, because Q3 failed.** The pre-registration also
  said a parity failure is a defect to report, not evidence for or against the rule. The diagnosis shows what it is:
  the live rows replay exactly (100% within 0.05R), and the engine differs from the evaluator by two conventions —
  it enters at the 4h close, which the live scan cannot (+0.03R), and it sets T1/T2 from the fill instead of
  the setup's fixed prices (+0.045R). Both make the engine look better than live; neither creates the
  result. At the live entry time the signal still beats random shorts by +0.43R per trade (CI clear of zero).
- **What the forward record is not:** clustering (62 independent bets, CI [+0.07, +0.49]) or "shorts went down"
  (random shorts on the same coins in the same weeks: −0.12R).
- **Why history disagrees (Q4, a hypothesis):** on the same coins and weeks, the engine's fires that the live scan
  also took made +0.33R (n 69); the ones it did not take made +0.03R (n 204). The live pre-filter (top 30 by
  interest score: turnover, OI, the 24h move, funding extremes, momentum hot list) appears to choose the coins
  where a failed breakout works. The fixed-universe backtests (365 d: −0.04R) never had that filter.
- **Caveats:** the rule was picked because its forward record was the best of ~7 (winner's curse); six weeks,
  one falling market; W42 (n 20) lost to random. The selection effect was found after looking.
- **Engine finding (separate from this rule):** `unified_backtest` enters at the bar close and sizes T1/T2 from the
  fill; `weekly_eval` enters after the scan and uses the setup's prices. ~+0.075R/trade optimism on this rule.
  One trade model everywhere (discipline #7) → backlog item.
